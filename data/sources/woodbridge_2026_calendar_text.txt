Town Around.”

The Recycle Coach APP again accompanies the 2026 calendar.  We made a number of improvements 
to the Recycle Coach APP that make the APP easier to navigate and simplifies trash and recycling 
collection schedules, special collection information and more – all right at your fingertips!!!  Search the 
database for specific recycling instructions. Keep track of upcoming special collection events, household 
hazardous waste drop-off, confidential document shredding day, Christmas tree collection, bagged leaf 
collection and more.  Report a waste problem or send comments and suggestions through the APP.  
We are also proud to report that the Woodbridge Township Department of Public Works including the 
Office of Recycling continues their day-to-day work providing outstanding service to all of our residents.  
In fact, it’s important to note that last year, the Office of Recycling earned $ 287,342.72 from the 
New Jersey Recycling Tonnage Grant which is based on the amount/tonnage of materials collected and 
recycled along with $ 230,710.00 from the New Jersey Clean Communities Grant.  The grant funding 
allowed for the expansion of the ReUse It Center at the Public Works facility in Keasbey and to further 
expand our recycling efforts to include the recycling of Styrofoam, cooking oil(s), plastic bags, film and 
shrink-wrap.   Throughout the year, the Department of Public Works will continue to implement many 
more sustainable initiatives and programs that serve to better manage the ways we offer services, 
conserve energy and other natural resources at work and home. 
For more information on all of the Department of Public Works services and programs, call the Sanitation 
Office at 732-738-1311 x3600 or the Office of Recycling at 732-738-1311 x3035.  Download the Recycle 
Coach APP or visit the Township Web Page at www.twp.woodbridge.nj.us for all the information 
regarding the Automated Trash Collection & Recycling Program.

Throughout the winter months, the Department of Public Works is 
dedicated to keeping our roads clear and our residents safe. Primary 
roads are plowed and salted first to ensure emergency and main travel 
routes remain open, followed by all secondary and residential streets. 
 
Residents can help by: 
• Removing vehicles from the street when snow is predicted, and 
keeping them off until plowing is complete. 
• Never shoveling or blowing snow into the street, it creates icy 
hazards. 
• Waiting until plows have passed before clearing driveway aprons. 
• Avoiding unnecessary travel during storms to allow plows to do their 
job safely and efficiently.

Know Your Flood Hazard: Protect Yourself and Your Property from Flooding
Protect Our Natural Floodplains
Only rain goes down the storm drain. The ability for storm drain systems to function correctly directly relates to minimizing flood inundation, potential damage sustained during strong rain events, and the reduction of non-point source 
pollution into our waterways. To learn more about the beneficial functions of natural waterways and floodplains...check out the Township floodplain management webpage: 
https://www.twp.woodbridge.nj.us/635/Floodplain-Management. Let’s keep our waterways and floodplains clean and pristine. Report illegal dumping! Call: 1-877-WARN DEP 
and/or Woodbridge Twp Division of Engineering: 732-602-6057.
Know Your Flood Hazard
Woodbridge Township has areas of special flood hazards located in the AE, A, Coastal A, and VE zones. You can locate Special Flood Hazard Areas in your neighborhood by looking at the Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRM) at FEMA’s 
Map Service Center or on the Township’s floodplain management website, via the Woodbridge Township Interactive Flood Viewer which shows both the 2010 Effective FIRM and 2014 Preliminary FIRM, or by visiting our FEMA Flood 
Zones link on the Township’s floodplain management webpage. Woodbridge Township contains areas of tidal and fluvial flooding. Never drive down flooded roadways. For your safety, our web pages have links to real-time tide gauge 
data. The Township of Woodbridge participates in the Middlesex County Hazard Mitigation Plan. The plan is designed to help identify local weaknesses and assess ways to potentially combat those issues. Thus, the overall outcome 
of the plan’s development is to reduce devastating effects of future storm events and other natural hazards. The plan is available to view on the Township’s floodplain management website. Please note that the Woodbridge Township 
Flood Viewer should only be used as a reference. For further flood zone clarification or to request a free flood zone designation letter for your property, please contact Thomas C. Flynn, M.P.A., CFM in the Woodbridge Township Division 
of Engineering.
Protect Yourself/Property from Flood Hazards
This information has been developed to help minimize risk within floodplains, and in order to reduce the amount of flood damage sustained during future flood events. Some areas of Woodbridge Township have been historically 
susceptible to flooding, and as a result it’s important to know how to prepare for, respond to, and recover from a potential flood event. This information along with many other helpful flood preparedness and recovery tools can be found 
on the Township’s floodplain management website: https://www.twp. woodbridge.nj.us/635/Floodplain-Management.
Build Responsibly
If you are in the market to potentially purchase property, you should examine any possibility of a flood hazard BEFORE YOU BUY & BEFORE YOU BUILD, and the need to have flood insurance to protect yourselves and your 
property. Historic flood risk may not be the same risk you face today or in the future. By rebuilding higher or to a more resilient standard, you can reduce-or avoid- future flood loss and reduce the impact on your finances. The Township 
recognizes The New Jersey State Flood Hazard Area (FHA) Control Act Rules, N.J.A.C. 7:13. Please note that in situations where the base flood elevation (BFE) denotes a more protective delineation, the State of New Jersey is using 
the information as the basis for statewide floodplain development standards. To learn more about the types of N.J. Flood Hazard Area Permit Authorizations, visit our Floodplain Management website and to view the Township’s Municipal 
Flood Damage Prevention Ordinance, including Chapter 22 of the General Ordinance and the Township’s Open Space Conservation & Resilience Zone Ordinance (OSC/R Zone).
Carry Flood Insurance
Federal law requires that a Flood Insurance Policy be obtained as a condition of a federally backed mortgage or loan that is secured by the structure located in an area of special flood hazard, as per the Effective Flood 
Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) delineation. Although some areas susceptible to flood risk do not require the purchase of flood insurance, it is recommended in order to best minimize financial vulnerability during a potential flood event. 
It’s important to carry flood insurance even if you are not federally required to do so. According to FEMA, a structure located in a Special Flood Hazard Area has a 26% annual chance of experiencing a 1% annual 
exceedance probability flood(100-year flood event) over the course of a 30 year mortgage. A property not in a Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA) may be damaged by a flood greater than that predicted on a flood study, 
such as a FIRM or state delineation. According to FEMA nearly 25% of all National Flood Insurance Program flood insurance claims come from policy holders outside of federally mapped Special Flood Hazard Areas. Learn more about 
how the Township’s FEMA Community Rating System Program can help to potentially reduce your flood insurance premiums through the NFIP, even for structures mapped outside the SFHA. 
        
FEMA COMMUNITY RATING SYSTEM – WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW
The Woodbridge Township Division of Engineering, in conjunction with the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), implements the Community Rating System. For detailed FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) 
determinations based on a street address which delineate floodways, limits of wave action, state protected natural areas, NOAA sea-level-rise data, historical extents of flood inundation, and flood depths contact the 
Woodbridge Township Division of Engineering for assistance: 732-602-6057. In addition, the Township offers flood protection assistance information in order to help property owners reduce vulnerability from flood related 
hazards. Flood-proofing techniques vary in size, scale and scope in relation to specific flood hazard conditions and the use of the structure. Some retrofitting alternatives might include elevating the structure, constructing engineered 
flood walls and protecting utilities from flood vulnerability. This service is intended to help provide one-on-one assistance to property owners in order to evaluate potential flood protection options. This service is not intended to function 
as means by which to offer design, engineering, or construction project support. Interested parties should contact the Division of Engineering in order to arrange for a consultation. Be sure to obtain all necessary permits before 
construction. NOTE: Consultation is based on the available “Flood Insurance Rate Maps(s)” of the municipality. This consultation does not imply that the referenced property will or will not be free from flooding or damage. A property 
may be damaged by a flood greater than that predicted on the FIRM or from a local drainage problem not shown on the map. This consultation does not create liability on the part of the municipality, or any officer, or employee thereof, 
for any damage that results from reliance on consultation determinations.
If you have questions about flood insurance policies or for detailed FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) determination based on a street address contact the Woodbridge Township Division of Engineering for personal 
assistance: 732-602-6057 
Tips to be better prepared for potential flooding events
*Be aware of alternative evacuation routes from your neighborhood.
*Gather supplies that you might need during a flood event, such as: a “go-bag” battery-powered radio, 
emergency kit, etc.
*Plan for how you will care for your pets.
*Create a plan. Visit www.redcross.org to learn more about developing ways to be better prepared.
*Avoid downed power lines that could pose a serious life-threatening hazard. Report these issues 
to utility companies immediately.
*The Township conducts stream channel inspections annually and in relation to strong storm events. 
If you have a conveyance concern, you may call the Division of Engineering at: 732.602.6057.
*Generators should never be operated inside your home or garage.
Steer Clear of Flooded Roads
*Never drive on flooded roads - almost half of flood deaths happen in 
vehicles
*6 inches of water is enough to cause you to lose control of your vehicle
*If you encounter flood waters on a roadway Turn Around, Don’t Drown.
